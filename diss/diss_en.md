## 1. Theme urgency

The last decades are characterized by rapid development of technology,
economy and society, where cardinal changes
 are taking place (development of high technologies, population growth, global
climate change, etc.). These factors affect, among other things,
energy industry, placing new and new demands on it.

Among the most significant changes in the development of the energy
industry, scientists and researchers include the following [[1]](#ref1):
- growing shortage of primary sources for the production of electric energy;
- increasing demands on the part of consumers for reliability and quality of power supply;
- increasing cost of electric energy worldwide;
- stricter requirements for environmental and industrial safety of the operation of energy facilities;
- the need to reduce system-wide costs.

These changes require the development of a new concept of
innovative development of the electric power industry. This concept, on the one hand, should meet modern views, goals and values of
social and public development and be formed based on
expected needs of people and society as a whole. And on the other hand - to take into account the main trends and directions of scientific and technological progress
in all sectors, spheres of life and activities of society [[2]](#ref2) as much as possible.

Today, Russia's power grids are mainly based on
design solutions from the mid-20th century. This is one of the main reasons
for the complexity of grid infrastructure expansion, which is necessary to
meet the ever-growing energy needs. In
the next decade, electricity demand is expected to grow by
19%, while the existing grid infrastructure can only increase its
capacity by 6% [[1]](#ref1). For these reasons, blackouts and
power outages are becoming a problem for most
electrical grids.

The continuous rise in the cost of energy resources, the basis of which
are hydrocarbons - oil, gas, hard coal, etc., as well as
the growing complexity of their extraction are constraints for
the development of traditional energy infrastructure. In addition,
processing of hydrocarbon fuel resources has a negative
impact on the environment. Therefore, it becomes relevant to apply
renewable energy sources, both in large power grids,
and in small [[3]](#ref3).

<a name=p2></a>

## 2. Renewable energy sources

Renewable energy is based on the use of energy from the sun,
wind, water and other environmentally friendly resources. In the future, it will be able
to compete with conventional energy. According to forecasts
of the European Renewable Energy Council, the share of renewable
energy sources (RES) in the global primary energy production
will amount to 34.7% in 2030 and 2040. - 34.7%, in 2040. - 47.7%
<span class="mark">[[4]](#ref4).</span> The main disadvantage of RES is unstable
generation schedule, which depends on geographical location, time
of year, time of day, weather conditions and other factors [[5]](#ref5). Therefore,
when using RES, special so-called
“intelligent” systems are in demand, which can optimally utilize various
distributed energy sources.

One type of smart energy system is the
microgrid system. It is a low-voltage smart distribution network,
containing various distributed energy sources,
energy storages, controlled loads and other elements.

These features make it relevant to create a unified methodology
development and research of power supply of buildings, using
distributed energy sources with a significant share of generation from
RES.

## 3. The concept of the smart house

The term “intelligent home” (IH) has recently become very
popular. At the same time, different companies often interpret it in their own way.
There are a number of definitions, the essence of which can be briefly formulated as
a building that provides productive and cost-effective
by optimizing four elements: structure, life support systems,
maintenance, management and their interconnections.

There is a more general and comprehensive definition of this concept -
a set of integrated infrastructure functions.

Requirements to engineering systems of ID can be conditionally divided into two
groups: requirements of the personnel servicing engineering
systems of the building and requirements of users of engineering systems - owners
and tenants of premises. The first group defines engineering
infrastructure of the building, the second - the structure of comfort control systems and
interfaces of common engineering systems of the building.

The smart home systems are divided into engineering infrastructure,
technology systems and security systems.

The engineering infrastructure of a smart home includes
life support systems for personnel and the technological platform on which
other systems operate. Engineering infrastructure includes (see
Fig. 1): power supply system, heat supply system,
air conditioning and ventilation system, water supply and sewerage system.

The technological infrastructure includes: building information network,
communication and communication system. The security system includes: security
alarm system, fire alarm system, notification system

An important place among the engineering structures of a smart building is assigned to
clockification system, which should provide uniform time readings
on all timers connected to the system.

The purpose of the work is to find ways to improve the system of
power supply through the use of power sources based on RES.
The power supply system includes the following subsystems: general
power supply; guaranteed power supply; uninterrupted
power supply; lighting system.

There are many requirements to the power supply system, one of the
main ones is uninterrupted power supply. In the “traditional”
power system the issue of continuity is solved by
using the principle of “two independent sources”, which, as
operation experience has shown, has a number of disadvantages. The definition of
“independent” source, which is given in the Rules of device
of electrical installations has one significant disadvantage - it allows
the possibility of a short-term loss of power for the time of operation
of automatic reclosure devices (ARS) on the damaged
source or automatic switching on the backup source (ATS) in case of
unsuccessful ARS. Firstly, this technology initially assumes the presence of
dead time pause for the time of operation of automatic reclosure-ARR, and secondly, it relies on
a number of assumptions that may not work. For example, the success of reclosure depends
on the probability that a fault, such as a short circuit on
supply lines, will be self-liquidating after the voltage is removed
. According to statistics, successful reclosure occurs on cable transmission lines
in about half of the cases. ATS will be successful if the backup
source is guaranteed to be in working order. One of the ways
to increase the continuity of power supply to individual consumers,
for example office smart building, is the combined
use of centralized and autonomous power supply system.
This method is not new and has been used for a long time for power supply of
especially critical facilities. A diesel generator is usually used as an additional source
. The system turns out to be quite
expensive.

In recent years, an innovative concept
of building power supply systems, called microgrid, has become widespread. It
also assumes joint operation of the municipal network and autonomous
sources, but as the latter are used various devices,
based on obtaining electricity using RES technology.

This is facilitated by the following factors: rapid development of technology
of energy conversion of solar, wind, biogas and other renewable
sources of primary energy, affordability of autonomous
solar, wind, etc. power plants and creation of modern,
programmable, digital devices for monitoring, control and protection of these
objects. An important feature of modern autonomous systems for obtaining
electricity based on RES is the possibility of their synchronous operation with
network. The advantages of such a technical solution for the consumer -
the possibility to sell surplus electricity to the grid, and for the power system
- the construction of a system with distributed generation.

It is known that on the way from the generator of a modern large thermal,
nuclear or hydroelectric power plant to the end consumer 380/220V
electric power undergoes 8-10 stages of transformation. This is inevitably
accompanied by technological power losses. In the presence of
distributed generation the number of transformation cycles is reduced, and
electricity can be consumed practically at the place of its receipt. This
reduces losses on electric power transportation. At a certain
concentration of RES, some sections of the utility grid get the property
of being practically supplied with local electricity during certain
hours of the day, for example, on a sunny day.

Such fragments of a large power system containing distributed
generation are called Microgrid - which means microgrid or
microgrid. Microgrid is an integral part of a more global
concept of innovative development of the electric power industry - Smart Grid [[2]](#ref2).

The societal effect of microgrid, as well as the expansion of RES application,
is to save fossil fuel and energy resources, and
as well as to reduce harmful emissions into the environment in the process of their
processing in the production of electricity.

## 4. The structure of Smart Home power supply

The sources of distributed generation, according to the Smart Grid [[2]](#ref2) concept, can contain various
technologies such as wind turbines, photovoltaic cells (solar panels),
microturbines, diesel or gasoline generators, electricity storage
(batteries). The main ( basic) source of power supply remains
electric network. The capacity of distributed generation sources can range from several kilowatts to megawatts.

Increasing the degree of autonomy in the distributed generation system
is proposed to be realized with the help of agent-based approach. An agent is understood as a physical or virtual object,
acting in the system. In our case agents can be considered both
sources of distributed generation listed above and their
control systems. In general, the Microgrid control system is a
multi-agent complex with its own central controller.

Let us consider the possibility of applying the agent-based control principle in
Microgrid system. Each agent has a certain level of autonomy,
which makes it possible to make decisions without a central controller or
coordinator. To this end, they are programmed to operate using
set of options. For example, a battery charge controller
will accumulate and charge the battery if the battery
low; there is a surplus of cheap energy from RES, the cost
of electricity on the grid in that time slot of the day is low
low price per kW\*h. Thus, the multi-agent system decides when
to start charging the battery based on its own rules and goals, and
not by external command.

Each agent's autonomy depends on the resources available to it and which it
can currently use. These resources can be: fuel
for an internal combustion engine (in the diagram of Fig. 2 it is an internal combustion engine), wind
sufficient to run a wind turbine, or sunlight for
a photovoltaic converter. The multi-agent system should be
flexible enough with feedback elements. For example, a wind generator
by changing the energy output can change the parameters of other agents,
change the voltage level on the busbars of the power system and thus
influence the operation of the source “electric grid”. From a more
profound point of view here we can talk not only about economic
indicators when paying for consumed electricity, but also about the safety
of the power supply system as a whole, affecting the level of short circuit currents
and the stability of the system in case of a short circuit.
